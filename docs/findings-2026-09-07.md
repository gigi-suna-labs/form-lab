# FORM — findings so far (Sept 7, 2026)

Analysis of the 13 recordings in `sessions/` collected Sept 4–5. Written for Gigi and Suna to share one picture before the real data sprint.

## Bottom line

- The stretch sensor reliably registers muscle contraction. **Solid.**
- The IMU separates a curl from a front raise, and curls from both people cluster together. **First real evidence the exercise-ID approach works. Promising, tiny sample.**
- Rep counting was overcounting badly; the rebuilt detector is now within one rep of the true count on every labelled set. **Fixed, still approximate.**
- Load (weight) does not yet show a clear signature. **Inconclusive.**

## 1. The sensor works

Every active recording shows clear resistance excursions well above a quiet, stable rest, across two people, two days, and both bench and on-arm setups. The basic transduction — stretch tracks contraction — is not in doubt.

Absolute resistance is **not** comparable between sessions: rest ran ~660 Ω on the bench to ~1000 Ω with the band on the arm, and drifts as the rubber relaxes. Everything must be measured against a per-session moving baseline, which is how the app computes it.

## 2. The IMU separates exercises (the important result)

All five curl recordings (both people) cluster together; the one front raise sits clearly apart.

| recording(s) | gravity axis | rotation dominant on | rotation energy (gyro RMS) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Curls — 5 recordings, both people | y, ≈ -0.96 | y-axis | low, 20–31 |
| Front raise — 1 recording | spread across x/y/z | z-axis | high, ~71 |

Two people's curls landing on top of each other while a different exercise separates cleanly is the go-signal for the classifier approach. It is one front raise, so it is a strong hint, not a proven boundary.

## 3. Rep counting: rebuilt

The first detector fired on every wobble: your "bicep 5 curls" counted **12**. The rebuilt detector smooths the signal (0.35 s), tracks a moving baseline, and counts a rep only when the signal crosses a high line and returns below a low line (hysteresis), with a minimum gap and duration.

Accuracy on the labelled sets after the rebuild:

| recording | true | detected |
|---|---|---|
| four hand pulls | 4 | 3 |
| bicep 5 curls | 5 | 4 |
| suna curl none x5 | 5 | 5 |
| suna curl medium x5 | 5 | 5 |

Within one rep everywhere, down from wildly wrong. Good enough to use, not yet production accuracy. Parameters will be re-tuned once more data exists.

Caveat: cord-based rep counting only works when the muscle **under the band** contracts. A front raise with the band on the bicep barely moves the cord, so reps for such exercises must come from the IMU, not the cord.

## 4. What does not hold yet

- **Load / weight.** Medium-weight curls gave a slightly bigger bump than bodyweight, but with one recording each it is a hint, not a finding.
- **Anything statistical about a classifier.** The sample is tiny and inconsistent: 5 curls, 1 front raise, 1 messy session, mixed placements, several clips only 2–9 s, person label missing on some early ones. Not enough to train or validate.

## 5. What to collect next

Run the recording protocol properly. The priorities that turn "promising" into "validated":

1. **Consistent placement** — board on the arm every time, same orientation.
2. **Full 10-rep clean sets**, 3 s still at each end.
3. **Both people**, and a second day, to test person-independence.
4. **At least three contrasting exercises** — curl, front raise, shoulder press — to confirm the clusters hold and stay separate.
