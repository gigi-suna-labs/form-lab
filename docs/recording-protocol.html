Recording protocol, for Gigi and Suna

What to record so a program can learn which exercise you're doing and how hard the muscle is working. Same setup every time, one exercise per recording, and one deliberately messy session per gym visit.

The one rule that matters

Same setup, every time. Band on the right bicep, and "band on" set to right bicep in the form. Board riding on the upper arm, USB port pointing toward the shoulder. Band snug enough that two fingers slide under, no more. The motion sensor is on the board, so where the board sits decides what "up" and "forward" mean. If the board is loose or upside down, the data from that day can't be compared with any other day.
  1. Put the band on. Check the board orientation. Check nothing dangles.
  2. Open FORM Lab. The dot in the corner should be green.
  3. Fill the form: person, exercise, load, reps planned. Notes if anything is unusual.
  4. Press Record. Stand or sit still for 3 seconds. This gives every recording a calm start to measure from.
  5. Do the set. Count reps out loud so you know the true number.
  6. Still for 3 seconds. Press Stop & save.
  7. If anything went wrong, open the recording, set quality to "bad, ignore", save. Don't delete it.

The labels, and what they mean

Every recording gets a small label card saved beside its data. The app fills it from the form. You can edit it later by opening the recording and pressing Save labels.

Eight classes

Six real exercises the band on the bicep can plausibly tell apart, plus two that exist to teach the program what "not a curl" looks like. Start here. Don't add classes until these are solid.

classwhat to dowhy it's in
bicep curlpalms up, elbow pinned, full rangeThe core case. Big cord bump, forearm rotates up.
hammer curlpalms facing each other, same motionNearly the same motion as a curl. Tests whether the sensors see grip and muscle differences.

lateral raisestraight arm out to the side to shoulder heightRotation around a different axis, almost no bicep.
front raisestraight arm forward to shoulder heightLooks like a lateral raise to the cord, differs only in motion direction. Good hard pair.

squatbodyweight or goblet, band still on the armThe arm is passive. Teaches the program that whole-body movement is not a rep.

Squat is the only leg exercise. Everything else in the gym that isn't on this list goes into the messy session, not into its own class.

What to record: the clean sets

One recording per set. A set is 10 reps at a steady pace, about 2 seconds up and 2 seconds down, with a 3-second still period at each end. Each person does every class at two loads, and then repeats the whole thing on a different day.

"Light" and "heavy" mean what they mean for you. Pick the weights on day one, write them in the notes field, and use the same ones on day two.

Add one extra curl set per day at a deliberately slow tempo, 4 seconds up and 4 down, labelled with "slow" in the notes. It tests whether time under tension is measured, not just counted.

The messy session

Clean sets teach the program what exercises look like. They don't teach it what everything else looks like, and in a real gym everything else is most of the time. So once per gym visit, record one long session where you just train, with the band on, and drop marks as you go.

Choose exercise messy gym session in the form, press Record, and leave it running for 8 to 12 minutes. During it, type a word in the mark box and press Enter whenever something changes. Marks are just timestamps with a word. They don't have to be perfect, and it's fine to type them a second or two late.

A script that covers the ground, in roughly this order:

  1. Walk to the dumbbell rack. walking
  2. Pick up weights, carry them, put them down. carrying
  3. Check your phone for 20 seconds. phone
  4. A set of curls, don't count carefully, just train. curl at the start, end after.
  5. Rest a minute. Fidget, drink water, fix your hair. rest
  6. A set of a different exercise from the list. press or whichever, then end.
  7. Re-rack the weights. carrying
  8. Something not on the list at all: stretching, a plank, a cable machine. other
  9. One more set of anything. Mark it. end.
  10. Walk away, sit down, press Stop & save.

The words to use for marks, so they're consistent: walking · carrying · phone · rest · water · other · end, plus the class names curl · hammer · press · lateral · front · row · tricep · squat.

The "other" class, on purpose

Besides the messy session, record a few short deliberate "not exercising" clips, labelled other / not exercising with the activity in the notes. About a minute each. These are the things most likely to fool a rep counter.

  • Sitting still, then standing still
  • Walking normally, arms swinging
  • Scrolling a phone with that arm
  • Drinking from a bottle, five sips
  • Putting on and taking off a jacket
  • Picking up a dumbbell from the floor and putting it back, five times, no reps
  • Adjusting the band itself

Seven clips per person, once. About 15 minutes total.

When to mark a recording bad

  • The band slipped or was re-tightened mid-set.
  • The board came loose or rotated on the arm.
  • The green dot went out during the set (board disconnected). The recording will be short.
  • You lost count and don't know the true rep number.
  • Someone talked to you and you stopped halfway.

Bad recordings are still useful later for seeing what failure looks like, so mark them, don't delete them.

What we'll do with it

  1. After day one, I lay the eight classes side by side and we look at whether the motion traces separate by eye. That's the go / no-go for the approach.
  2. Each rep gets cut out using the cord peaks, and turned into a handful of numbers: which way gravity pointed at the start and at the top, which axis rotated most, how long the rep took, how tall the cord bump was.
  3. A simple classifier on those numbers, trained on day one, tested on day two and on the other person. If it holds above 90 percent on five or more classes, the idea is proven.
  4. The messy sessions then test the harder question: can it stay quiet when you're not exercising, and wake up when a set starts.
