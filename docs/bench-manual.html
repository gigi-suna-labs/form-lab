Step 4 · The one move you repeat 14 times

Soldering is: hot pen touches pin, feed in wire, pull away

Before heat: clear the table. Put a ceramic plate next to you for the iron to rest on. Plug the Pinecil into the Mac charger, press the button nearest the tip, set it to about 330. Only ever hold the black handle.
Step 5 · Order

Two corners first, then stop and send a photo

  1. Solder the two circled corner pins.
  2. Put the iron on the plate. Send a photo.
  3. When I say go, do the remaining 12.
  4. Hold the Pinecil button to turn it off. Leave it 5 minutes to cool.
Step 6 · Seat it for real

Pull it out, push it back in across the gap

The gap down the middle of the breadboard is a wall. Rows on one side don't touch rows on the other. The XIAO needs to sit across that wall so its two rows of legs land on different sides. Otherwise the left legs and right legs share rows and everything is shorted together.

Check: every leg should have at least one empty hole in line with it, on the outside. That empty hole is where a wire goes to reach that leg.
Step 7 · Find the three legs you need

A0, GND, 3V3. Ignore the other 11.

The names are printed on the XIAO next to each hole, in tiny letters. Hold it with the USB port pointing away from you and use this map.

Step 8 · Wire the cord

Three wires, two clips, one resistor

Rubber can't be pushed into a breadboard, so the cord connects through alligator clips. Everything else is jumper wires pushed into holes.

  1. Push one end of a jumper wire into a free hole in line with the A0 leg. Leave the other end loose.
  2. Push one end of a second jumper wire into a free hole in line with the 3V3 leg. Other end loose.
  3. Cut a 4-inch piece of cord. Put an alligator clip on each end, the way you did for the meter.
  4. Clip the A0 wire's loose end with cord clip 1. Clip the 3V3 wire's loose end with cord clip 2.
  5. Resistor: one leg into a free hole in line with A0, the other leg into a free hole in line with GND. It has to reach across, so bend the legs. If you have no resistor, use a second cord piece with two more clips and two more wires, exactly like steps 1 to 4.
  6. Look: no clip touching another clip, no bare metal touching bare metal except where you meant it.
Update · Sept 7 · connection method

Alligator clips won. Keep them.

We tried skipping the clips: stripping a jumper wire and wrapping the bare copper around the end of the cord. On the bench it looked fine, but in use it was a disaster. The wrap kept loosening, and the reading drifted and dropped out. The alligator clip biting the cord end is the most reliable connection we have, so that stays the design.

  1. Cut the cord to the length you want between the two contact points.
  2. Clip an alligator clip onto each end of the cord, the same way you did with the meter.
  3. Clip the other jaw of each lead onto a jumper wire, and push those wires into the A0 row and the 3V3 row. The resistor still bridges A0 to GND.
For the wearable: tape or stitch each clip down to the band so it can't shift or twist as you move, and slip heat-shrink over the clip-to-wire joint. That fixes the one weakness of clips (getting knocked loose) without giving up their reliable grip.
Step 9 · Plug in and read

The numbers appear on the Mac

Plug the XIAO into the Mac with the USB-C cable and tell me "ready". I upload the logging program from here. Then open Arduino IDE, go to Tools, then Serial Plotter, and set the speed in the corner to 115200. You'll see this:

If the line does something else, this is what it means:

  1. Jumps around wildly, never settles. A0 isn't connected to anything. Check wire 1 is in a hole in line with the A0 leg, and that its clip is really biting the metal.
  2. Flat at -1. A0 is touching GND directly. A clip or resistor leg is in the wrong row.
  3. Flat near 0. A0 is touching 3V3 directly. The cord clips are probably touching each other.
  4. Reads but the number jumps when you touch a clip. Contact, not cord. Same lesson as the meter. Squeeze the clips on tighter.

Sept 4 finish line · reached

Checklist

  1. Headers placed, XIAO seated across the gap (not soldered yet, and it still worked)
  2. Wire from A0 row → clip → cord → clip → wire from 3V3 row
  3. 10 kΩ resistor from A0 row to GND row
  4. Program loaded with the "XIAO nRF52840 Sense (No Updates)" board, graph moves when you stretch
  5. Rest ~780 Ω · stretched ~1650–1860 Ω · drift at rest ~10 Ω per 20 s · slow relax after a pull
Two rules learned the hard way: always pick the board named "(No Updates)". And a red blinking LED means rescue mode, not a running program: one tap on RST restarts the program, two taps enters rescue mode.
Next session · the body test

Put the cord on your arm and do a curl

This is the test the whole idea depends on. A muscle gets thicker when it contracts. If the band stretches with it, the graph should show one bump per rep. Prep steps 1 to 3 before you sit down with the laptop.

  1. The band is made. A strip of the black elastic with hook-and-loop (velcro) sewn to the ends so it wraps the upper arm and closes. Sewn, not stuck, so it survives the pull of every rep. Reuse it each session.
  2. Attach the cord. Cut a fresh 4-inch piece of cord. Clip an alligator clip on each end, then stitch or tape each end to the elastic about 4 inches apart, middle free, and tape the clips down so they can’t shift.
  3. Wear it. Band around the bicep, snug but comfortable, cord along the front where the muscle bulges. The two wires hang down to the breadboard. Sit close to the laptop, keep slack in the leads.
  4. Record. Plug in, say "ready", I start a 60-second recording. Hold still 5 seconds, do 5 slow curls, hold still.
  5. Read it together. We're looking for 5 bumps. Five bumps means FORM's core idea works on a real arm.
Soldering the three pins is optional before this. Everything worked unsoldered on Sept 4. Do it whenever.
After the data sprint · cutting the cable

Going wireless: Bluetooth and a battery

Today the board sends its numbers up the USB cable. The XIAO you have already has Bluetooth Low Energy built in, so the cable can go. Four pieces, in order. Only the battery is something to buy.

  1. Firmware. The board advertises itself over Bluetooth and streams the same ten numbers, 20 times a second. Software only. It can be tested while the board is still on USB power.
  2. FORM Lab. A "Connect over Bluetooth" button next to the USB status. Chrome can talk to Bluetooth devices directly from the page, so nothing else changes.
  3. Battery. The XIAO has a built-in charger and two pads on its underside marked B+ and B-. It charges whenever the board is plugged into USB, so no separate charger is needed. See the shopping list and the wiring below.
  4. Leave the breadboard. Short wires soldered to A0, 3V3, and GND, the cord and resistor clipped to those. Same circuit, small enough to sit in the band with the battery.

What to buy for the battery

Two parts. The board has the charger built in, so that's all. Under 20 dollars total.

partwhat to get
LiPo batterySingle-cell 3.7 V, ~400 mAh, with a JST-PH 2.0 mm plug and built-in protection (PCM). Size code 502035 is 5 × 20 × 35 mm, thin enough for the band.
JST-PH 2.0 pigtailThe mating connector with loose wires, to solder to the board. Kits come with both male and female halves so one of them fits the battery.

Not needed: a separate charger (the USB-C port charges it), and anything for Bluetooth itself (the antenna is on the board and the firmware is done).

Fitting the battery

  1. Try plugging each connector from the kit into the battery. The one that clicks in is the mate; solder that one, set the other aside.
  2. Check polarity first. Plug your chosen connector into the battery, touch the multimeter to the two loose wires, and find which reads positive. Generic batteries sometimes wire the plug the opposite way, so never assume.
  3. Solder the positive wire to B+ and the negative wire to B- on the board's underside. Two dots, same move as the headers.
  4. Flash the Bluetooth firmware (firmware/form_logger_ble.uf2) the drag-and-drop way: double-tap RST, drop the file on the XIAO-SENSE drive.
  5. Plug in the battery. In FORM Lab, connect over Bluetooth instead of USB. Charge by plugging the board into USB-C.
Battery safety: a LiPo can catch fire if punctured, crushed, or shorted. This one has built-in protection, but still don't fold or pierce it, and buy from a seller with real reviews.
Timing: keep USB for the data sprint with Suna. It's reliable and never runs flat. Order the battery now, the Bluetooth firmware and button get built in the meantime, and switching over is one afternoon once the recordings are in.
